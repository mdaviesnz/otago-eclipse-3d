Otago Eclipse 3D

Total solar eclipse · 22 July 2028

On the afternoon of 22 July 2028 the moon's shadow crosses Otago. Totality lasts up to about three minutes — but the sun will be only 8–10° above the horizon, roughly one fist held at arm's length. At that angle a single hill in the wrong place is enough to hide it completely.

This tool answers one question: from where you are standing, will terrain be in the way? It flies you to a spot in 3D, works out the sun's exact position for that location, and traces the sightline across real elevation data to the horizon.

Using it

  • The verdict — choose a place and it tells you whether terrain hides totality there.
  • Watch it happen — plays the eclipse through, with the landscape darkening.
  • Best viewing spots — precomputed places across the path of totality, filterable by how you can get there.
  • Map — all of those spots on one overview map.
  • How the astronomy works → — a plain-language guide to the eclipse geometry and calculations (with a link to the full technical specification for astronomers).

Independent, unvalidated tool — treat results as a guide, not a guarantee. This is an independent project developed with AI assistance and has not yet been independently validated by an astronomer or surveyor (see the technical specification if you can help review it). Elevation data also has limits, and being near a mapped road is not a right of access — check any spot in person before the day, and never look at the partially eclipsed sun without proper eclipse glasses.
